# When premium grew

Growth is only a story when it is set against the market. A carrier that grew 6% while
Marsh's placements grew 12% lost ground; say so.

1. **Growth against the market first.** Carrier movement next to the Marsh market movement
   for the same scope and period, and what that did to share of Marsh placements.
2. **Name what carried it.** Use the `driver.*` fact: the line (or industry, country)
   behind most of the gain, and its share of it. Concentrated growth is a strength to
   protect and a dependency to watch — say which, if the evidence shows it.
3. **Check the rank.** Did the carrier move up among the carriers placed through Marsh?
4. **Look for what did not grow.** A line that fell while the total rose is worth one
   bullet, because it is the question the carrier will not ask.
5. **Where next.** Headroom or whitespace facts show where the next point of share is
   worth the most.

No superlatives without a cited ranking. "Strong growth" says nothing; "grew twice as fast
as the Marsh market" says something.
