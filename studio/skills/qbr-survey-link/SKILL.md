# Survey scores next to premium

The Marsh carrier survey records how Marsh colleagues rate the carrier, by practice (line)
and by question area. When a `survey.line.*` fact is in the evidence, it lets the ICL say
whether the premium story and the relationship story point the same way.

- **Pair like with like.** Only put a survey score next to a premium movement for the SAME
  line — the fact names it. "Casualty premium fell $4.2M, and Casualty is also the practice
  whose survey score slipped most (3.6, down 0.3)."
- **Corroborate, never explain.** A lower score and a lower premium in the same line is a
  pattern worth raising, not a proven cause. Say "also", "at the same time", never
  "because".
- **Use the score's own scale and movement** exactly as rendered in the fact.
- **A strong score in a weak line is a finding too**: the relationship is intact, so the
  question is capacity or appetite — put it as the question.
- Mention the survey once or twice per page at most; it supports the premium story, it
  does not replace it.
