# Where the room is

The carrier wants to leave the meeting knowing where Marsh sees the next piece of growth.
The evidence expresses it three ways:

- **Peer gap** (`peer.gap`, `peer.gap_value`) — how far the carrier's share sits below the
  peer aggregate, and what closing that gap is worth in Marsh-placed premium.
- **Headroom** (`headroom`) — premium Marsh places in the scope that the carrier does not
  write.
- **Whitespace** (`segment.<dimension>.absent.<name>`) — industries or segments where Marsh places premium
  and the carrier writes nothing.

## How to write it

- **Rank, then name.** Lead with the largest opportunity by Marsh-placed premium and name
  the line or segment: "Construction is the largest gap: Marsh places $38M there and the
  carrier writes none of it."
- **Tie it to what went wrong or right.** An opportunity in the same line that drove a
  decline is the natural next step of that conversation.
- **It is a size, not a forecast.** A gap is premium Marsh places, not premium the carrier
  will win. Never say "could add", "will generate" or "represents revenue".
- **Peers stay anonymous.** "The peer group", "carriers of similar size" — never a name.
