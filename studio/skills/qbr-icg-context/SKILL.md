# The meeting this deck is for

ICG is Marsh's **Insurer Consulting Group**. ICG provides analytics and consulting to
insurance carriers. Every quarter an **Insurer Consulting Leader (ICL)** sits down with a
carrier's leadership and walks them through how the carrier performed with Marsh over the
period. The deck is the ICL's script for that conversation.

The conversation has a shape, and the commentary serves it:

1. **How the carrier performed with Marsh** — premium placed through Marsh, its movement,
   the carrier's rank and share of Marsh placements, against the Marsh market over the same
   period.
2. **Where it came from** — which lines, industries, client segments or countries moved
   the result. A total without its driver is a scoreboard, not an insight.
3. **Where the carrier stands against its peers** — always as an aggregate peer group,
   never a named competitor.
4. **Where the room is** — the parts of Marsh's placements the carrier is under-weight in,
   or absent from, measured in Marsh-placed premium.
5. **What to talk about next** — the questions the numbers raise for the carrier's
   leadership. The meeting, not the slide, is where causes and plans are agreed.

## What the reader already knows

The carrier's executives know their own business. They do not need the definition of
premium, and they do not need the slide read back to them. They need the ICL's reading of
the numbers: what is material, what is surprising, and what is worth their time in the
room.

## What the data can and cannot tell you

The evidence covers **Marsh-placed premium**, share of Marsh placements, rank, the peer
aggregate and — when the deck draws on it — the Marsh carrier survey. It does **not** cover
the carrier's own initiatives, pricing, appetite, renewal outcomes, claims or external
market events. Never state a cause the evidence does not contain. Where a movement begs a
cause the data cannot give, frame it as the question the ICL will put to the carrier.
