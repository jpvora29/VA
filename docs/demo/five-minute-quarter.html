The opening

Hook · What you get · Scope · Money

BeforeScreen showing the Setup page, untouched. Do not start on a slide.

One question before I show you anything. Last quarter, how many working days did this team spend building QBR decks? (beat — let someone answer) Almost none of that was analysis. It was assembly. Pull the extract. Rebuild the same chart. Retype last quarter's paragraph with new numbers. Then check it three times, because one wrong figure in front of a carrier costs you the room.

So here is what you get. If you present: the story is ready the day the numbers land, not three weeks later. If you build: you stop being a copy-paste machine. If you are the carrier: you get your portfolio, benchmarked, with commentary you can challenge — because every sentence points at the figure behind it.

Today it runs on our own data. Premium and the carrier survey. By country, product and year. Peers set market by market. Straight into our template. Next comes any template, your own data, an analyst you can ask questions to, and a meeting that writes itself up.

The money is simple. [N] people. [H] hours a deck. [D] decks a quarter. That is [N×H×D×4] hours a year that becomes minutes. And that is before the rework you don't do when a number changes in week three.

Why this lands: you have priced their current way of working in their own numbers before showing a single feature. Everything after this is them buying time back, not buying software.

Minutes of Meeting

The part everybody hates

ShowTranscript in → structured minutes out. Point at owners and dates.

Every QBR ends the same way. Someone offers to write it up. It arrives four days later, and the promises have gone soft. This takes the recording and gives you decisions, owners and dates, in our format, before the room empties. Actions you can chase beat notes you can file.

Deck recap

Sixty pages → one

ShowFinished deck in → one-page recap out.

Last one. That deck is sixty pages, and your CEO will read one. This reads the finished deck and writes that page. The story. The three numbers that matter. The talking points for whoever presents it. Same deck. Thirty seconds of prep instead of an evening.

07 5:30–5:50 20 sec ~55 words

The close

Land it. Then ask.

Five minutes ago we had an empty form. Now we have a full deck, commentary checked line by line, an analyst you can question, minutes, and a one-page recap. The quarter takes twelve weeks. Reporting on it should not take three.

What I need is one market, one carrier for next quarter, a data owner for a day, and your template. Give me those and you will see your own deck come out of this. Not mine.

Why this lands: a small, specific, dated ask converts. "What do you think?" does not. Stop talking after the ask.

Objections, answered in one breath

Keep every answer short. Long answers sound defensive.

They sayYou say
"Can I trust AI with our numbers?"The model never produces a number. It writes sentences around figures the engine calculated, and every claim is checked back against the data. If it cannot be traced, it never reaches the page.
"Our template is different."Good. The template is an input, not a limit. You give it yours, it fills yours.

Pre-flight — 15 minutes before

A five-minute demo has no room to recover. Every item below has ruined one.

  1. Pre-generate the deck in a second tab. Click Generate, then cut to the finished one. Never let the room watch a spinner.
  2. Warm the cache and run the chatbot question once, so the first live answer is not the slow one.
  3. Pick a carrier with a story — clear growth in one market, a clear weakness in another. A flat portfolio makes a good tool look boring.
  4. Check the peer set is filled for the market you demo, and that no competitor name is visible anywhere.
  5. Close every other tab. Notifications off. Zoom up one notch. Match the theme to the room's projector.
  6. Pre-load the transcript and the recap output. Those segments are thirty seconds each. They cannot survive a file dialog.
  7. Say the opening question out loud first. If the hook falls flat, the next five minutes are just a feature tour.
  8. Know your one ask and say it last. Market, carrier, template, data owner, next quarter.
